Parts of the sample

The sample is a tree of parts: each part is one shape of one material; a part inside another replaces its material there (an inclusion, a hole, a grain).

    What holds the sample

    The holder is chosen apart from the sample and placed for you, under it or around it, from its size. Only its size and material can change. It is saved with the sample (and attenuates the beam where it is in it).

    Fit the sample to your measurements

    Some numbers of your sample (a density, a fraction, a thickness) are adjusted until the transmission it predicts matches the one you measured, wavelength by wavelength, in a region of the image: the values come with their uncertainties. It uses the files of your proposal measured with the Timepix3 and the choppers (time of flight).

    Your measurement and your sample

    Left, the transmission you measured (all the wavelengths); right, what your sample predicts over the same field. Draw the region of the fit on either picture (drag a rectangle).

    Choose the files above, then .

    The region of the image

    Where the transmission is measured (mm, the center of the field is 0): a region crossed by the part you fit, the same everywhere inside it. Drawn on the pictures above, or typed here.

    What to fit

    Tick the numbers to fit, between the bounds. Materials are fast (about half a minute); shapes and positions are slower (the sample is rebuilt at each step). Two numbers that do the same thing (the thickness and the density of one part) cannot both be fitted: the fit says when they are correlated.
