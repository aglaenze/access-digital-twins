The liquid

Small-angle scattering sees particles through their contrast: the difference Δρ between their scattering length density ρ and that of the liquid around them. The liquid is a mixture of light water (H2O, ρ = −0.56) and heavy water (D2O, ρ = 6.36·10⁻⁶ Å⁻²): its D2O fraction sets its ρ, and its hydrogen gives a flat incoherent background.

Kinds of particles

As many kinds as you want, dispersed in the liquid; dilute, so that each particle scatters as if alone and the scattering of the kinds adds up. Sizes in ångström (1 nm = 10 Å).

What holds the sample

At LoKI a liquid sample sits in a cell of fused quartz: two flat windows, the liquid between them. The windows are in the beam (they remove a few % of it); their own small-angle scattering is weak and flat, and the twin neglects it.

The cell

To scale along the beam. Only the gap (the thickness of liquid) can change: the windows are those of the twin's model (2 × 1.25 mm of fused quartz).

Fit the sample to your measurements

The sizes and the amount of your particles are adjusted until the curve they predict matches the I(Q) you measured (your sample minus the solvent, in absolute units): the values come with their uncertainties. It uses the files of your proposal: the sample, the solvent alone and the empty beam.

What to fit

Tick the numbers to fit, between the bounds (a flat background is always fitted with them: what the subtraction of the solvent leaves). A fit takes from a few seconds to a minute.
